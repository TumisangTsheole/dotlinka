Building a C2C (Consumer-to-Consumer) platform is all about **trust** and **scannability**. Because the content (images and descriptions) is generated by users and can often look messy, your UI needs to be the "clean container" that holds it all together.

Here are proven, industry-standard guidelines to get you started without needing a design degree.

---

### 1. Typography: The "Safe" Stack
For e-commerce, readability is more important than "flair." You want a sans-serif font with a large x-height (the height of lowercase letters).

*   **Primary Suggestion:** **Inter** (Free on Google Fonts). It was designed specifically for computer screens and looks professional at any size.
*   **Secondary Suggestion:** **Roboto** or **Public Sans**.

**Font Scale (The "Modular" Approach):**
Stick to these sizes to ensure hierarchy:
*   **H1 (Page Titles):** 32px | Bold | Tight line-spacing (1.1)
*   **H2 (Product Titles):** 24px | Semi-bold
*   **Body Text:** 16px | Regular | Line-spacing (1.5 or 1.6 for readability)
*   **Secondary/Meta Text:** 14px | Regular (used for dates, categories, or "Sold by")
*   **Micro Text:** 12px | Medium (used for tags or status badges)

---

### 2. The "Trust" Color Palette
C2C platforms need to feel neutral so the product photos pop. Follow the **60-30-10 Rule**:

*   **60% Neutral (Backgrounds):** Use an "Off-White" (`#F8F9FA`) for the page background and pure White (`#FFFFFF`) for cards and sections. This creates a subtle depth.
*   **30% Secondary (Text & Borders):** Use a "Soft Black" (`#1A1A1B`) for text and a very light grey (`#E5E7EB`) for borders. Avoid pure black text; it’s too high-contrast and causes eye strain.
*   **10% Primary (Action):** Pick **one** brand color for "Buy" buttons, links, and active states. 
    *   *Safe bet:* A deep Blue (`#2563EB`) or a Forest Green (`#16A34A`). Both signal reliability.

---

### 3. Borders and Rounding
Sharp corners look "technical" and "dated." Round corners look "friendly" and "modern."

*   **The Sweet Spot:** **8px (`0.5rem`)**.
    *   Use 8px for product cards, buttons, and input fields.
    *   Use 4px for smaller elements like tags or badges.
    *   **Consistency is key:** If your buttons are rounded, your images must be too.

---

### 4. Layout & Spacing (The 8pt Grid)
Designers use a "base-8" system because most screen resolutions are divisible by 8. It makes your site feel "mathematically" aligned.

*   **Padding/Margins:** Use increments of 8 (8px, 16px, 24px, 32px, 64px).
*   **Line Spacing:** For body text, set your `line-height` to **1.5**. If your font size is 16px, your line height should be 24px.
*   **Product Cards:** Use a subtle box shadow instead of a heavy border. 
    *   *Example:* `0 4px 6px -1px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.1)`.

---

### 5. C2C Specific Tips
*   **The "Empty State":** Since users might not have uploaded photos yet, have a clean "No Image" placeholder using your neutral grey.
*   **Search Bar:** Make it the most prominent thing on the landing page. Give it a slightly larger rounding (12px or fully pill-shaped) to make it feel "tappable."


The "Facebook Marketplace" vibe is what designers call **Utilitarian UI**. It isn't trying to be a luxury brand; it’s trying to be a tool. It prioritizes speed, familiarity, and "content over container." 

Since you aren't a designer, the best way to achieve this is to lean into **System Fonts** and **High-Density** layouts. This makes the app feel like a native part of the user's phone or browser.

---

### 1. Typography: The "Native" Feel
Facebook Marketplace uses system fonts. This is a pro-move because you don't have to load external files, and it feels "correct" on every device.

*   **The Font Stack:** 
    `system-ui, -apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, "Segoe UI", Roboto, Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif;`
*   **Font Weights:**
    *   **Prices:** Bold (`700`). In C2C, the price is the most important piece of data.
    *   **Titles:** Semi-bold (`600`).
    *   **Descriptions:** Regular (`400`).
*   **Sizes:**
    *   **H1 (Headers):** 20px (Keep it relatively small to save space).
    *   **Product Title in Grid:** 14px or 16px.
    *   **Price:** 16px (Bold).
    *   **Location/Timestamp:** 12px (Secondary grey color).

### 2. The Marketplace Palette
You want a "Clean Blue" brand. Blue is psychologically tied to trust and community—essential when buying a used lawnmower from a stranger.

| Element | Hex Code | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Brand** | `#1877F2` | "The Facebook Blue." Use for buttons and active icons. |
| **Background** | `#F0F2F5` | Off-white/Grey background. Makes white product cards "pop." |
| **Surface** | `#FFFFFF` | Pure white for the actual product cards. |
| **Primary Text** | `#050505` | Almost black for headers and titles. |
| **Secondary Text** | `#65676B` | For "2 miles away" or "Posted 4h ago." |
| **Border/Divider** | `#CED0D4` | Very thin lines to separate sections. |

### 3. Border Rounding (The "Friendly" Factor)
Facebook moved from sharp corners to rounded ones a few years ago to feel more "social."

*   **Product Cards:** `8px` or `10px`. 
*   **Buttons:** `6px` (keep them slightly "squarer" than the cards to look more like a clickable tool).
*   **Search Bar:** `20px` (The "pill" shape is the universal symbol for "Search" now).

### 4. Layout: The "Grid & Card" System
The secret to the Marketplace look is **Information Density.**

*   **The Card:** Don't use heavy borders. Use a thin `1px` border in `#CED0D4` or a very soft shadow.
*   **Image Aspect Ratio:** Use a **1:1 (Square)** ratio for images. C2C photos are usually messy; forcing them into squares keeps the grid looking tidy even if the photos are bad.
*   **Spacing:** Use a **12px or 16px gutter** (the gap between cards). Anything wider feels too "airy" for a busy marketplace.

---

### 5. Proven Design "Must-Haves"
*   **The "Sticky" Header:** Keep the search bar and category filters at the top of the screen when the user scrolls. In C2C, users "doom-scroll" through items; don't make them scroll back up to search.
*   **The "Message" Button:** Since this is C2C, the primary action is usually a conversation. Make the "Message Seller" button full-width and your primary brand color on the product page.
*   **Location Badges:** Always show the general area (e.g., "Midrand • 2km") right under the price. Distance is the #1 dealbreaker in C2C.

### A "Wildcard" Suggestion
If you want to look a bit more "modern" than Facebook but keep that same trust level, try using **Inter** as your font and bump your primary blue to a slightly deeper shade like `#2563EB`. It feels a bit more "Tech Startup" and a bit less "Social Media."
